HOW IT WORKS

How to calculate overtime pay

Overtime pay starts with your regular hourly wage and the overtime multiplier that applies to your situation. A common example is 1.5×, often called time and a half.

Hourly wage × overtime multiplier = overtime hourly rate

Once you know the overtime rate, multiply it by the number of overtime hours worked.

Overtime hourly rate × overtime hours = overtime pay

Example: $25 per hour with 5 overtime hours

If you earn $25 per hour and your overtime multiplier is 1.5×, your overtime rate is $37.50 per hour. Five overtime hours would produce an estimated $187.50 in overtime pay.

If you work the same five overtime hours every week for 52 weeks, that would equal an estimated $9,750 in annual overtime earnings before taxes and deductions.

TIME AND A HALF

What is time-and-a-half pay?

Time and a half means your overtime hourly rate is 150% of your regular hourly wage. In calculator terms, the multiplier is 1.5.

Regular hourly wage × 1.5 = time-and-a-half rate

For example, if your regular rate is $20 per hour, your estimated time-and-a-half rate is $30 per hour. If your regular rate is $30 per hour, the estimated rate is $45 per hour.

DOUBLE TIME

How to calculate double-time pay

Double time uses a 2.0× multiplier. To estimate double-time earnings with this calculator, enter 2 in the overtime multiplier field.

Regular hourly wage × 2 = double-time hourly rate

For example, a regular wage of $25 per hour would produce an estimated double-time rate of $50 per hour. Four hours at that rate would equal $200.

IMPORTANT

Overtime rules and eligibility can vary

This calculator is designed for general estimation and does not determine whether you are legally entitled to overtime. Overtime requirements can differ based on location, employer policies, employment classification, occupation, schedule, and other circumstances.

The results are estimates of gross overtime pay. They do not subtract income taxes, payroll taxes, benefits, insurance, retirement contributions, or other deductions.

If you want to combine regular wages, overtime, bonuses, commissions, tips, and other earnings, use the Gross Pay Calculator.

FAQ

Overtime pay calculator FAQ

What does time and a half mean?

Time and a half means an overtime rate equal to 1.5 times your regular hourly wage. For example, $20 per hour multiplied by 1.5 equals an overtime rate of $30 per hour.

How do I calculate overtime pay?

Multiply your regular hourly wage by your overtime multiplier. Then multiply the resulting overtime rate by the number of overtime hours you worked.

Can I calculate double time?

Yes. Enter 2.0 as the overtime multiplier. For example, a $25 hourly wage at double time equals $50 per overtime hour.

What is my overtime rate if I make $20 an hour?

At a 1.5× multiplier, a $20 regular hourly wage produces an estimated overtime rate of $30 per hour.

What is my overtime rate if I make $25 an hour?

At time and a half, $25 per hour becomes an estimated overtime rate of $37.50 per hour.

Does this calculator include taxes?

No. The results are gross-pay estimates before taxes, deductions, benefits, and other payroll adjustments.

Is overtime always paid at 1.5 times the regular rate?

Not necessarily. Overtime rules and rates can vary. Check the requirements that apply to your location and employment situation.

Can I change the number of weeks worked per year?

Yes. You can enter between 1 and 52 weeks to estimate annual overtime earnings for seasonal work, unpaid time off, or other schedules.
