HOW IT WORKS

How to calculate a freelance or contract hourly rate

A practical client rate starts with the annual income you want to earn, adds the business expenses your client revenue must cover, and divides that total by the hours you can realistically bill during the year.

Break-even hourly rate = (Target annual income + Annual business expenses) ÷ Annual billable hours

How to calculate a contract hourly rate

If you work as an independent contractor, your client billing rate is not necessarily the same as an employee hourly wage. Your rate may need to cover non-billable time, equipment, software, insurance, unpaid time off, administrative work, and other business costs.

For rate-planning purposes, estimate the annual income you want the work to support, add annual business costs, and divide the total by realistic billable hours.

Why billable hours matter

Freelancers and contractors usually cannot invoice every hour they work. Proposals, marketing, bookkeeping, client communication, learning, and administrative work can reduce the hours available to bill clients. Using realistic billable time prevents your rate from being based on an unrealistic 40 billable hours every week.

Example freelance and contractor rate calculation

Suppose your annual income goal is $80,000, annual business expenses are $10,000, and you expect to bill 25 hours per week for 50 weeks. That gives 1,250 annual billable hours and a break-even rate of $72 per hour.

($80,000 + $10,000) ÷ (25 × 50) = $72/hour

Adding a profit or risk buffer

A buffer can help account for slow periods, scope changes, unexpected expenses, or profit beyond your minimum income target. Numviro applies the percentage you enter to the calculated break-even rate.

Freelance rate vs employee hourly wage

An employee wage and a freelance or contract billing rate answer different questions. An employee wage is compensation for work performed as an employee, while a client rate may need to fund the contractor's business costs and non-billable time as well as income.

FAQ

Freelance & contract hourly rate calculator FAQ

How do I calculate what to charge per hour as a freelancer?

Add your target annual income and annual business expenses, then divide by realistic annual billable hours. Add a buffer if you want the rate to include additional profit or protection against downtime.

How do I calculate a contract hourly rate?

Add the annual income you want the contract work to support to the business costs your client revenue must cover. Divide that amount by realistic annual billable hours, then add a buffer if needed.

Why can a contractor hourly rate be higher than an employee hourly wage?

A contractor billing rate may need to cover business expenses, non-billable work, equipment, insurance, unpaid time off, and other costs that are not represented by an employee hourly wage.

What are billable hours?

Billable hours are the hours you can actually charge to clients. Administrative work, marketing, proposals, and other internal work are often non-billable.

How do I calculate a freelance or contractor day rate?

Multiply your recommended hourly rate by the number of billable hours you plan to include in one day.

Should business expenses be included in my freelance rate?

If client revenue needs to cover those expenses, including them in the rate calculation helps prevent them from reducing your target income.

Does this calculator include taxes?

No. Tax obligations vary significantly by location and circumstances, so this calculator focuses on income, expenses, billable time, and an adjustable pricing buffer.
